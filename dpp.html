What the passport must carry, by category

The fields below are what buyers and regulators ask for first in each sector. Keystone collects each one through supplier requests, from the tier that actually holds it, once. It lands in a single product record that also feeds your other obligations — EUDR, LCA and the rest — so nothing is collected twice.

How firm the requirement is varies by sector. These are ordered by how soon a passport is actually due:

Mandated legal date setIn draft delegated act expected, scope not finalNot on the roadmap no DPP requirement confirmed

Batteries Mandated · 18 Feb 2027

Required under the EU Battery Regulation ((EU) 2023/1542, Art. 77) from 18 February 2027 — the first DPP with a hard legal date, and the only one that applies to each individual item.

  • A passport for every individual battery above 2 kWh, each with its own unique identifier, from 18 February 2027
  • Keystone tracks each battery at unit level and generates its QR data carrier, as the Regulation requires
  • Chemistry, capacity, state of health and expected lifetime
  • Carbon footprint and recycled content for cobalt, lithium, nickel and lead
  • Supply-chain due diligence on the critical raw materials

Textiles & apparel In draft · ~2027

A priority group in the ESPR working plan. The textile delegated act is expected around 2027, with compliance roughly 18 months later; the exact fields are not yet final.

  • Fibre composition per component, with recycled content and its basis (GRS, mass balance)
  • Chemical compliance across the wet processing chain (REACH, ZDHC)
  • Durability, care and repair information
  • Country of key manufacturing steps

Furniture & wood In draft · ~2028

A priority group in the ESPR working plan, with a delegated act expected around 2028. The DPP fields are not final yet — but the EUDR wood data below is already due now.

  • Wood species and origin, tied to EUDR geolocation and legality evidence
  • FSC or PEFC chain-of-custody claims, with the certificate on file
  • Substances of concern in finishes and foams (REACH, formaldehyde)
  • Repairability, spare parts and disassembly for end of life

Electronics & EEE Priority · no date set

Named among ESPR priorities, and some electronics already carry ecodesign information rules, but a DPP delegated act and date are not yet set.

  • Material composition and RoHS restricted substances at component level
  • Spare-parts availability and software-support period
  • Energy performance and recyclability, WEEE routing
  • Safety conformity: CE, LVD, EMC evidence

Cosmetics & personal care Not on the roadmap

Not named in the current ESPR working plan, so no DPP requirement is confirmed for cosmetics yet. The fields below reflect the product and packaging rules you already manage.

  • Full formulation and INCI, with the CPNP notification reference
  • Allergen declarations and claim substantiation
  • Packaging composition and recyclability for PPWR
  • Animal-testing and vegan status where claimed

Your sustainability expert, on your side

Think of it as a sustainability expert working alongside your team, keeping evidence collection up to date for you.

How Keystone builds it

  • Category templates set the required fields, so you collect exactly what the delegated act asks for
  • Supplier requests pull each field from the tier that holds it, upstream where needed
  • Public and restricted views, with a QR data carrier generated from the record
  • Keystone hosts each passport at a stable, resolvable address and registers its unique identifier in the EU DPP Registry (ESPR Art. 13); the registry indexes the identifier, while the passport data stays with you, on Keystone
  • When a material changes, the passport updates from the same record, not a new project
