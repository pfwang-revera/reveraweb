Keystone for Manufacturing

Know which product information applies before work begins.

A revised drawing arrives. A material substitution is discussed by email. An updated supplier document reaches one team but not another. Manufacturing then has to establish what applies to the order in front of it.

The challenge is not always a lack of information. It is the absence of a clear connection between the product definition, the revision and the records used downstream.

Product information needs continuity

NIST’s digital-thread work focuses on connecting product and manufacturing information across the lifecycle. The underlying idea is relevant even before a business attempts a large digital transformation: the information used at one stage should remain intelligible at the next.

Source: NIST: Digital Thread for Manufacturing

For a production team, that continuity starts with identifiable products, components, specifications and changes.

Bring the relevant context together

Keystone provides a shared foundation for product and supplier information. Connect the BOM with the applicable specification references, supplier records and supporting documents. Where batch-level work is in scope, agree how those records relate to the relevant production or sourcing references. Manufacturing can then approach a question with the product context already assembled.

Treat substitutions as a cross-team decision

A substitute component may appear equivalent for one purpose while changing something another team relies on. The dimensions may fit, but the material declaration differs. The supplier may be approved, but the evidence covers another facility. The product may require updated care information.

A shared record makes it easier for the responsible people to review those implications together. The product-data workflow should complement existing production planning and execution systems, with their respective responsibilities agreed.

A useful first application

Choose one product family with recurring specification questions. Map the approved BOM reference, the supplier information needed before production and the documents used during a quality review. Identify where people currently stop to seek clarification. Then establish a consistent handoff: what changed, which products it concerns, who reviewed it and where the current information can be found.

Real-time feedback on batch data

When a batch is produced, its data does not have to wait for a report. Captured against the shared product record, batch results are visible to design and QC as they happen, shortening the feedback loop from weeks to the same shift.

A problem spotted early, in the batch rather than in a complaint, is far cheaper to fix. Shorter loops mean fewer defective runs, less scrap and rework, and faster corrective action, which is money saved on every line.

Look for fewer interruptions

Measure clarification time, incomplete handoffs and recurring information-related rework. Keep the baseline specific so the team can see whether the new process helps.

Start with the production question that repeatedly sends people back to email. Connect the records needed to answer it before the next handoff.
