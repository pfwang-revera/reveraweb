Keystone for Marketing

Make the product story as dependable as the product itself.

You have a launch date, a compelling story and several channels to prepare. What you do not always have is one agreed set of product facts.

The website uses an earlier material description. The dealer presentation contains an old percentage. A sustainability statement has supporting evidence, but nobody can quickly confirm which products it covers. Marketing is expected to make the message simple; first, the team has to make the information consistent.

The product can become a continuing customer touchpoint

GS1 describes how a product-linked digital experience can connect customers to information and services through a scan. That creates an opportunity beyond the initial campaign: useful information can remain connected to the product throughout ownership.

Source: GS1 US: Digital Link for brands

For marketing, the question becomes: what would make a customer glad they opened this page? Before purchase, it might be materials, finishes and the making of the product. After purchase, it may be care instructions, repair guidance or a route to the right replacement part.

The passport as your always-on product channel

The consumer Digital Product Passport gives marketing a page on every product, reached by a scan, that you control. Because it is built from the same record as the regulatory passport, the facts are always current.

  • Tailor content, language and look and feel by market.
  • Add modules customers find useful: care, repair, refill, takeback, spare parts, registration and feedback.
  • Synced live with product data, so a changed material updates the story everywhere at once.
  • Built-in analytics show which content and features people actually use.

Build the story on reviewed facts

Keystone connects product information with its source context. Marketing can work with the relevant teams to distinguish:

  • Factual specifications from broader claims.
  • Current information from superseded content.
  • Evidence that supports a statement from evidence that merely appears related.
  • Public information from confidential supplier detail.

Approved information can then feed the relevant channels through agreed workflows and integrations.

Give customers a reason to engage

The published &tradition showroom story describes Keystone-powered passports that connected selected designs to information about materials, components, suppliers and production. It demonstrates a product-information experience, not a measured sales uplift.

Source: Studio 9: Powering DPPs for &tradition

A useful next step is to connect that experience to customer intent: discover a finish, download a specification, find a dealer, or access care guidance. The information should help the customer do something.

Keep learning after launch

Measure which information people seek, which actions they take and where they still ask for help, using appropriate analytics arrangements. Those signals can guide better content and expose recurring gaps in the product record.

Start with one product whose story deserves a richer experience. Connect the facts, review the claims and choose the next action that genuinely helps the customer.
