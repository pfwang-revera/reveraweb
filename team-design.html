Keystone for Design

Build the product once. Stop rebuilding its information for everyone else.

The design work does not end when the specification is approved. Someone needs a material breakdown. Another team asks for component quantities. A supplier changes a detail. Sustainability needs an updated input. Marketing wants to know which description is current. Design becomes the help desk for information it has already created.

Keystone helps that information become a reusable foundation rather than a series of one-off answers.

A BOM is useful beyond the original design task

A bill of materials connects a product to the components and materials that make it. The same structure can support sourcing, assessment inputs, evidence collection and product communication. The teams need different views and reviews, but they often begin with the same underlying relationships. This is consistent with NIST’s work on connecting design and downstream manufacturing information through a digital thread.

Source: NIST: Digital Thread for Manufacturing

Keep design ownership clear

Keystone can connect with PLM through an agreed API scope, using the relevant identifiers and BOM structure. The goal is to make the approved information useful to other teams while retaining clear ownership of specifications and revisions. Design should not have to maintain a separate spreadsheet merely because another department needs a different output.

See a change in its wider context

Consider a new upholstery choice. Design defines the material and performance requirement. Supply chain obtains the supplier’s information. Sustainability reviews the relevant quantities and composition. Compliance checks the supporting evidence for its purpose. Marketing prepares the approved description. With shared component identities, those activities can connect around the same product instead of creating separate versions of it. The result is a more coherent handoff, with review responsibilities visible.

Protect time for actual design

Revera estimates up to 70% less manual data work in &tradition’s design workflow through the shared-data approach. This is an estimate supplied by Revera, not a measured customer result or a forecast for every implementation.

Your own starting point should be the interruptions: how often another team asks for information already available, how long a specification clarification takes and where repeated entry occurs.

Time to market is the real payback

Every week saved between an approved design and a shippable product has a value: earlier revenue, a captured season, a competitor who does not reach the shelf first. When the same model and BOM serve every team, handoffs stop losing data and time, and the launch window moves closer.

Measured against that, the software pays for itself quickly: the cost of the platform is small beside the margin on products that reach the market weeks sooner.

Begin with a product that crosses several teams

Select one family with a manageable BOM and an upcoming change or launch. Agree the common identifiers, design-owned fields and downstream information needs. Establish how other teams can use the foundation without turning every request into another design task.

Bring one BOM and the questions people keep asking about it. Make the product knowledge travel with the product.
