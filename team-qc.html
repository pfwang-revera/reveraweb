Keystone for QC & quality

A test report is only useful when you know what it covers.

The report exists. That is the easy part. The harder questions are whether it relates to the correct product, component, supplier, facility, revision or period, and whether a subsequent change affects how it can be used.

When those answers are scattered, QC spends time reconstructing the context before it can make the quality decision. Keystone helps bring that context together.

Quality costs begin before a customer complaint

ASQ distinguishes the resources spent preventing poor quality, checking it, resolving failures internally and addressing failures after delivery. That makes evidence handling relevant to more than an audit: finding the right source earlier can help the team resolve uncertainty before it becomes another correction cycle.

Source: ASQ: Cost of Quality

The aim is not to collect the greatest number of reports. It is to know which records support the decision at hand.

Connect evidence to the thing being checked

Use the shared product structure to organise test reports, supplier declarations and inspection records. For each important record, establish:

  • The product, component or batch it covers.
  • The supplier and relevant facility.
  • The specification or requirement being assessed.
  • The report date, scope and any limitations.
  • The reviewer, decision and outstanding follow-up.

That makes retrieval a route to an answer rather than another search project.

Make the exception visible

Imagine a report relates to a component supplied from one facility, but a new order uses another. The existence of the report does not answer whether it remains suitable. The team needs to see the relationship, identify the gap and obtain the relevant review. Keystone provides the information foundation for that work; inspection decisions, sampling methods and release authority remain with the responsible quality process.

Carry learning into the next review

A recurring issue should not disappear into an email thread once the immediate order is resolved. Connect the finding and supporting records to the relevant product or supplier context. Make ownership of further action clear so the next reviewer can understand what happened.

Shared components mean shared QC

When products share a component, they can share its quality record. A test, a tolerance or a finding recorded once applies everywhere that component is used, instead of being rebuilt product by product, all managed in one place rather than a folder of PDFs.

  • When an issue is found, the right people are notified immediately, including the supplier responsible.
  • A finding connects to every product and batch affected, so a recall decision rests on the real spread of the component.
  • Verified quality data can flow to the Digital Product Passport and, where appropriate, to customers directly.

Works with your existing QC tool

Keep the inspection and testing software your team already uses. Keystone connects to it and brings results, test reports and findings onto the product record automatically, so there is no double entry and every QC record sits next to the specifications, suppliers and evidence it relates to.

  • Inspection results and test reports synced from your QC system, linked to the right product, component and batch.
  • One consolidated view of quality data alongside compliance and supplier data.
  • Less time searching, copying and reconciling, so reviews move faster.

Measure useful visibility

Track how long it takes to retrieve applicable evidence, how often reports arrive without adequate scope and how many findings remain without an owner. These are practical signals that the process is becoming easier to manage.

Choose one recurring quality issue. Bring the specification, the evidence and the records needed to establish what actually happened.
